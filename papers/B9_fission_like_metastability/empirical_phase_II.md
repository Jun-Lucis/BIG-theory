## Purpose

This follow-up asks how far the minimal BIG-B9 energy

[
E(Omega;lambda)=sigma P(Omega)+lambda C(Omega)
]

can be taken toward natural-physics comparison without turning it into a calibrated nuclear model.

The comparison is deliberately structural. The study tests whether a minimal competition between boundary cost and repulsive nonlocal interaction can generate a physically meaningful hierarchy of energetic preference, finite-amplitude coexistence, local loss of stability, and topology-changing pinch behavior.

## Negative and unresolved results retained

The release preserves the limiting results rather than regrading them:

- a limited two-case external barrier-magnitude check was mixed;
- U-239 was close to the simple frozen estimate, while Fr-210 was strongly underestimated;
- two prospective near-spinodal exponent tests failed to resolve a reliable local barrier exponent;
- the nonlocal quartic coefficient remained numerically unresolved under the tested Cartesian high-resolution diagnostics;
- no quantitative (lambda)-to-MeV calibration is claimed.

These results are part of the scientific conclusion.

---

## Interpretation boundary

The present model does not explicitly resolve shell corrections, pairing, tunneling, excitation dependence, fragment yields, or other nuclide-specific microscopic structure.

That limitation should **not** be converted into either of the stronger claims

[
	ext{“these effects are intrinsically non-boundary”}
]

or

[
	ext{“these effects must reduce to boundary variables.”}
]

The current result is narrower: the boundary degrees of freedom resolved by the minimal B9 model are sufficient to generate a nontrivial metastability hierarchy, but they are not sufficient for nuclide-specific quantitative reproduction.

Whether additional nuclear structure corresponds to deeper boundary structure, additional state variables, or a mixture of both remains open.

---

## Scope

BIG-B9 remains a finite two-dimensional reduced model. It is not a quantitative nuclear-fission theory and does not replace microscopic or macroscopic nuclear models.

The strongest current statement is structural:

> A minimal boundary-cost / nonlocal-repulsion model can separate global energetic preference from local compact-state stability and can generate an intermediate metastable regime.
