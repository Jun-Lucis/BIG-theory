# BIG-B9 Empirical Phase II: Metastability Hierarchies and Nuclear-Fission Structural Comparison

**Published preprint:**  
*Boundary Information Geometry B9: Metastability Hierarchies and an Empirical Structural Comparison with Nuclear Fission*

**Zenodo DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23128738  
**Publication date:** 2026-10-04

## Purpose

This follow-up asks how far the minimal BIG-B9 energy

$$
E(\Omega;\lambda)=\sigma P(\Omega)+\lambda C(\Omega)
$$

can be taken toward natural-physics comparison without turning it into a calibrated nuclear model. The study separates energetic preference, finite-amplitude coexistence, local loss of stability, and topology-changing pinch behavior.

## Retained scale hierarchy

| Model scale | Representative value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| far-separated energetic preference | $\lambda \approx 42$–$44$ | widely separated two-component branch becomes energetically favored |
| near-scission preference | $\lambda = 55.917826$ | near-scission/post-scission branch becomes energetically favored |
| smooth $m=2$ coexistence | $\lambda = 89.194319$ | circle and finite-amplitude smooth quadrupolar deformation have equal energy |
| smooth $m=2$ local spinodal | $\lambda = 89.739090$ | circular state loses local stability to the tested $m=2$ mode |
| $p=1$ pinch-envelope zero | $\lambda \approx 102.7$ | diagnostic topology-changing pinch envelope reaches zero |

The distinction is

$$
\text{global energetic preference} \ne
\text{finite-amplitude coexistence} \ne
\text{local stability loss} \ne
\text{pinch-envelope zero}.
$$

The coexistence and spinodal scales were reproduced in a prospectively frozen held-out-resolution test. P4 retains its prospective PASS. The reported 42–44 range is the spread of inherited self-energy and tail-fit estimates, not a proven bound or confidence interval.

## Empirical structural comparison

The descriptive normalization

$$
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\rm spinodal}}=\frac{X}{X_0}
$$

places a RIPL-3 subset of 52 nuclei with $88 \le Z \le 96$ on the model scale. All examined nuclei fall in the regime where separated configurations are already energetically preferred while the compact circular reference remains locally stable.

This is a structural placement, not a prediction of fission-barrier heights in MeV. The RIPL macroscopic cubic is a liquid-drop baseline, not a law that raw empirical barriers must follow isotope by isotope. This later analysis uses the larger tabulated barrier when two humps are available; the earlier comparison's five plotted values are specifically inner barriers and are not the same observable.

## Negative and unresolved results retained

- The limited two-case external barrier-magnitude check was mixed. U-239 was close to the frozen estimate; Fr-210 was strongly underestimated. The check was not pristine blind and had no predeclared PASS/FAIL threshold.
- Phase IIC P2 and P3 remain FAIL; P1 and P4 retain PASS.
- Two prospective near-spinodal exponent tests (IID P1/P2) remain FAIL.
- The quartic coefficient remains unresolved in the tested high-resolution diagnostics.
- No general quantitative $\lambda$-to-MeV prediction is established.

## Interpretation boundary

The minimal model does not explicitly resolve shell corrections, pairing, tunneling, excitation dependence, fragment yields, or other nuclide-specific microscopic structure. This does not establish either that these effects are intrinsically non-boundary or that they must reduce to boundary variables. Whether additional nuclear structure corresponds to deeper boundary structure, additional state variables, or a mixture remains open.

BIG-B9 is a finite two-dimensional reduced model. It does not replace microscopic or macroscopic nuclear models. Its current structural result is that a minimal boundary-cost/nonlocal-repulsion model separates global energetic preference from local compact-state stability and generates an intermediate metastable regime.
