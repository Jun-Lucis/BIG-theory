## Description
This programme-level synthesis integrates the evidence from BIG-B19 through BIG-B26, including the B19E external-validation branch and the B23A structural-universality audit. The paper distinguishes structural, formal, and quantitative universality and preserves negative, inconclusive, implementation-invalid, and transfer-failure outcomes as claim-defining evidence. B19E found a null/inconclusive incremental external predictive result under a frozen holdout while a separate local-budget audit closed the resolved operator bookkeeping to numerical precision. B20-B22 established finite-resolution local response geometry and prospective local prediction in restricted synthetic branches; B23 retained an inconclusive original aggregate verdict despite favorable branch-level evidence; B23A bounded stronger quantitative transfer claims across reconfiguration settings. B24-B26 independently showed that multiple native boundary classes can still admit a constructed family-restricted transfer relation, while a fixed coefficient that succeeded within one ellipse family failed prospectively in a valid topology-straddling two-center test. The retained programme-level conclusion is that BIG is better supported as a common formal language for system-dependent boundary geometry than as one universal quantitative boundary law.
