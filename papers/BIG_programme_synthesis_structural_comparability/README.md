# Programme Synthesis Draft

## From Quantitative Universality Tests to Structural Comparability in Boundary Information Geometry

**Status:** working manuscript draft; not a formal publication  
**Author:** Jun Lucis  
**Scope:** programme-level synthesis centered on B19–B26, with B3–B18 used as background context

This folder develops a synthesis paper whose central question is:

> What remains of BIG after stronger programme-wide quantitative-universality hypotheses are tested prospectively and partially fail, remain inconclusive, or prove family-dependent?

The manuscript is not intended to replace the individual B-series papers. It instead organizes their evidential relationship.

The main retained thesis is deliberately bounded:

> **Boundary Information Geometry is better supported as a common formal language for system-dependent boundary geometry than as one universal quantitative boundary law.**

The draft preserves negative, inconclusive, implementation-invalid, and geometry-transfer-failure results as part of the argument.
