# BIG Research Status Map — B3 through B26, with the B23A Universality Audit

## What is established, what is limited, and what is not supported?

This document is the current integrated evidential map of the **Boundary Information Geometry (BIG)** programme through B26, including the later **B23A structural-universality audit**.

It supersedes the older B3–B23 map **as the current repository overview**, while the earlier map remains preserved as the historical basis of the programme-level status note archived at:

- B3–B23 status-note DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22939024

Later phase records:

- B24–B25 Phase I: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22956894
- B25 Phase II: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22967474
- B26: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22972985
- B23A universality audit: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22994445

The word **established** is used only at the level of the stated reduced model, parameter family, finite resolution, and frozen numerical protocol. It does not mean a universal theorem or a calibrated empirical law of an external physical, biological, cognitive, or technological system.

The central distinction is:

```text
model-level result
    != programme-wide quantitative universality
    != universal theorem
    != empirical law of nature
```

## 2. Evidence architecture after B26 and B23A

The programme no longer reduces naturally to one line of increasing success. The evidence is better represented as several connected but distinct layers:

```text
B3–B8
local boundary formation / landing / finite-time stability

B9–B12
separation, capture, and non-assimilative post-capture states

B13–B18
observation-like updates, explicit history, readable history, boundary-dependent readout

B19–B23
finite-window channel structure and local operator-generated response geometry

B23A
prospective audit of how far that geometry/reconfiguration language transfers

B24–B26
separate cross-sector transfer programme:
multiple native boundary classes
    -> constructed level-resolved bridge
    -> family-restricted fixed coefficient
    -> geometry-transfer failure in a new two-center family
```

These layers are connected by a boundary-centered language. They are **not** currently derived from one universal equation or one quantitative invariant.

## 3. The strongest programme-level statement currently supported

The earlier B3–B23 map left open whether the programme might collapse into one common mathematical universality class.

B23A and B24–B26 sharpen that question.

The strongest retained formulation is now:

> **Boundary Information Geometry is a common formal language for comparing system-dependent boundary geometry, response, history, branch structure, and reconfiguration. Current evidence does not establish one universal numerical boundary law across the programme.**

This is more precise than either of the following stronger claims:

```text
"all BIG sectors obey the same operator"        — not established
"one small set of scalars transfers everywhere" — not established
```

At the same time, it is stronger than saying that the series are unrelated:

- local structures recur;
- several family-restricted predictors survive prospective holdout tests;
- branch identity and reconfiguration repeatedly matter;
- negative and inconclusive tests identify where quantitative transfer stops.

## 4. Universality ladder

The programme should now distinguish three levels.

### 4.1 Structural universality

Different systems may share an organization in terms of:

```text
boundary
response
history
branch
reconfiguration
```

without sharing the same coefficients or local geometry.

**Current status:** useful and consistent with the accumulated programme, but not a theorem that all systems possess these structures.

### 4.2 Formal universality

Different systems can often be written using comparable mathematical objects: boundary fields, response zero sets, local gradients/normals, history/readout maps, branch labels, and piecewise reconfiguration descriptions.

**Current status:** the strongest programme-level organizing claim.

### 4.3 Quantitative universality

The same numerical coefficient, scalar threshold, normal field, reset amplitude, locality contrast, or rate law would retain predictive value across different systems/families.

**Current status:** **not established**. B19, B23A, B24, and B26 all provide prospective or frozen-protocol limits on stronger quantitative transfer claims.

## 5. What has been falsified, limited, or left inconclusive?

BIG should retain these outcomes rather than treating them as temporary obstacles:

- B19 does not yield a robust tested ray-independent upstream scalar collapse.
- B20.6 remains **INCONCLUSIVE** because the frozen domain contains only one informative crossing.
- The B23 original aggregate plan remains **INCONCLUSIVE** because two endpoint-near stages were not executable under the original symmetric-window rule.
- B23A P1 does not support unchanged smooth-normal transfer to the new B9 family.
- B23A P4–P5 remain numerical inconclusives under their frozen resolution gates.
- B23A P6 does not support the frozen monotonic rate-ordering hypothesis.
- B24 does not identify one already-existing substantive operator shared by all audited sectors.
- B25.1 constant-density transfer fails.
- B25.2 is implementation-invalid rather than a valid transfer failure.
- B26 validly rejects the tested fixed-coefficient transfer to the new two-center family under its frozen accuracy criterion.

These results constrain the theory. They are part of the evidence, not results to be removed by retrospective tuning.

## 6. What remains genuinely open?

The principal open questions are now narrower than they were after B23.

### Mathematical / numerical

- Can formal boundary geometry be characterized by transformation or covariance rules that survive changes of coordinates and model family?
- Which quantities are branch-local invariants, which are family-dependent coefficients, and which are merely convenient coordinates?
- Can any finite-resolution transfer result be promoted to a controlled continuum statement?
- Are there new independently designed systems in which the same formal objects provide useful prospective predictions without retuning?
- Can the successful B25 level-resolved transfer be generalized without collapsing into a universal fixed coefficient that B26 has already shown to fail in the tested new family?

### External validity

Which, if any, BIG reduced structures survive after domain-specific equations, units, calibration, comparison with established theory, and empirical validation are imposed?

At present, BIG does not claim that this has been demonstrated.

> **BIG has established a sequence of reproducible boundary-centered structures and several prospectively successful local or family-restricted transfers in reduced models; subsequent frozen tests show that these successes do not justify one programme-wide quantitative boundary law, leaving formal/structural comparability as the strongest current unifying statement.**
