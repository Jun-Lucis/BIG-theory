# BIG Research Status Map — B3 to B23

## What is established, and what remains open?

This document is a compact evidential map of the Boundary Information Geometry (BIG) programme through **B23**.

The word **established** is used here in a deliberately limited sense. It means that a mathematical construction, numerical pattern, or prospective test has been demonstrated **within the stated reduced model, parameter family, finite resolution, and observation protocol**. It does **not** mean that the same statement has been proved as a universal theorem or validated as a quantitative law of an external physical, biological, cognitive, or technological system.

The central distinction is therefore:

```text
model-level result
    != universal theorem
    != empirical law of nature
```

## 2. The current hard core of BIG

The strongest common statement across the programme is **not** that “boundary = individual” has been proved as a law of nature.

A narrower statement is supported:

> **When boundaries are made explicit as dynamical fields, geometric costs, history-bearing interfaces, readout structures, or operator-generated response zero sets, reduced BIG models repeatedly produce reproducible local structure that can be measured and, in later stages, prospectively tested.**

The programme has therefore moved through four broad layers:

```text
B3–B8
boundary formation, landing, local/global stability, anisotropy

B9–B12
separation, capture, and non-assimilative post-capture states

B13–B18
observation-like updates, explicit history, memory-bearing boundaries, readout

B19–B23
boundary-core channels, operator-generated response geometry,
local kinematics, curvature, prospective and cross-branch prediction
```

These layers are connected by a common boundary-centered research language, but they are **not yet derived from one unified mathematical theorem**.

## 3. What is still genuinely hypothetical?

The central open mathematical question after B23 is:

> **Are the recurring structures in B3–B23 different manifestations of one common mathematical universality class, or are they only a family of useful but distinct reduced models?**

In particular, it remains open whether the following objects can be derived from a shared formal core:

- quadratic-like boundary landing in B3–B4;
- anisotropy-controlled finite-time thresholds in B7–B8;
- boundary-cost / nonlocal-repulsion competition in B9;
- retained-history and readout operators in B14–B18;
- direction-dependent channel families in B19;
- operator-generated response zero sets and their local geometry in B20–B23.

A second open question is external validity:

> **Which, if any, of these reduced structures survive after domain-specific equations, units, calibration, and empirical validation are imposed?**

At present, BIG does not claim that this has been shown.

---

## 4. One-sentence status

A compact current formulation is:

> **BIG has established a sequence of reproducible boundary-centered structures in reduced mathematical and numerical models; what remains open is whether these structures belong to a common mathematical universality class, and whether any of them survive as quantitatively valid descriptions of external systems.**

---

## 5. Why B23 is a natural pause point

B23 completes a coherent response-geometry arc:

```text
B20  response boundary
  -> B21 local kinematic transport
  -> B22 Hessian / curvature / normal rotation
  -> B23 cross-branch short-horizon transfer
```

This makes B23 a useful point at which to separate two future directions:

1. **confirmation / hardening** — new held-out families, endpoint-safe protocols, spatial and temporal convergence, independent replication;
2. **unification / abstraction** — identifying the smallest common mathematical structure linking the earlier boundary, history, readout, and response-geometry series.

The first direction increases evidential strength.  
The second asks whether BIG has a common mathematical core beyond a sequence of related reduced models.
