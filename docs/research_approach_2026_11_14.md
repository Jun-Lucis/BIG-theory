# BIG research approach: boundaries as an exploratory lens

*Public research-methodology clarification — 14 November 2026. Not a new scientific verdict, a revised mathematical theorem, or a finalized ontology.*

Boundary Information Geometry (BIG) is an exploratory programme that asks what becomes visible when boundaries, interfaces, responses and their histories are considered explicitly alongside bulk descriptions. It arose partly as a counterpoint to explanations focused only on bulk properties. It is **not** the claim that all phenomena reduce to boundaries, nor a rejection of established bulk physics.

## Questions before conclusions

A boundary perspective can help explain a particular phenomenon, fail to add explanatory value, or reveal a question that requires further examination. All three outcomes matter. When an initial boundary interpretation fails, BIG may examine whether the definition of a boundary, geometry, preparation, interaction, history, observation or time evolution changes the question. This does **not** authorize redefining a failed model until it passes: new assumptions belong to a distinct model and historical verdicts are preserved.

The programme distinguishes:
- **Philosophical questions:** individuality, non-assimilation, history-conditioned transformation, and the different significance that an entity may have in different relationships.
- **Mathematical questions:** what specified state spaces, equations, observables, estimates and counterexamples prove.
- **Physical questions:** what independently measurable, calibrated effects predict observations beyond equally informed conventional explanations.

The phrase “boundary as individuality” is a philosophical starting point, **not a demonstrated universal theorem**. Whether relational significance is created by an interaction or reveals an existing possibility is an open philosophical question. It is not settled by a measurement functional.

## Observation is not intervention

A passive observation map such as `O_q[u]` can distinguish states under one observation relation but not another without changing the state. An *active intervention* changes the preparation, boundary conditions or dynamical state and must be studied as a separate causal claim. Neither kind of result automatically proves what an entity means philosophically.

## Evidence discipline and present boundaries

For each mathematical or scientific claim, identify the equation/version, hypotheses, observables, prior mathematical results, conventional comparator, and the exact frozen assessment. Preserve `PASS`, `FAIL`, `INCONCLUSIVE`, `BLOCKED`, `NOT_RUN`, and new-assumption distinctions, including unsuccessful earlier tests.

- **BIG-MATH-01 v1.1** is a separately published, unreviewed positive-flux mathematics preprint (13 November 2026): [version DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24182121](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24182121). Its four main statements have an internal `PASS_WITH_SCOPE` audit; the novelty review classifies one as a direct consequence of existing theory and three as applications of existing mathematics. This is neither independent external peer review nor physical validation.
- **BIG-MATH-02** is ongoing mathematical exploration of free boundaries, numerical certification and relation-indexed observability. Static observation examples must not be presented as dynamic PDE existence theorems. The 1D mixed-flux weak-existence gate and general unknown continuous-front certification have not been established in the reported work.
- The **BIG-B42 overall scientific assessment remains `SCIENCE NONE` (independent R4 0/0/0)**. BIG-specific added physical predictive value over adequately informed conventional theories has not been independently demonstrated.

Philosophy and mathematics inform each other in both directions: a conceptual question may motivate a theorem, and a counterexample may change the philosophical question. Neither discipline is treated as a device for confirming the other's preferred conclusion.
